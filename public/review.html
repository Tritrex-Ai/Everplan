Prototype review · 9 July 2026

Everplan

A live-updating timeline and shot-list tool for wedding and event photographers — built and deployed, with real role-based access for everyone on the job.

everplan-rust.vercel.app
The pitch

One timeline, updated live, for everyone on the job — instead of a PDF nobody can find.

A photographer builds the day's timeline, keeps a private shot list against it, and runs a live Now/Next/Later board on the day itself. Second shooters, vendors, and the couple or client all look at the same source of truth — scoped to exactly what they're allowed to see, enforced at the database, not just hidden in the UI.

End to end

The core flow, start to finish

This is the loop every event goes through, from the first sign-in to the day itself.

1

Sign in and create an event

Any photographer can sign up and create their own events — a wedding, a brand shoot, anything with a schedule and a shot list. They become that event's owner automatically.

2

Build the timeline

Add blocks by hand, or describe the day in plain language and let the AI builder draft a structured timeline — editable before anything is saved. Creating an event drops straight into this step.

3

Build the shot list, privately

Every shot attaches to a timeline block. Shots are private by default — visible only to the person who made them — until they're explicitly marked shared.

Private by default
4

Invite the team

The owner invites people by email — as a Team member who can build their own shot list, a Vendor, or a Client — and can generate a no-login QR link for anyone who just needs to see the live timeline.

5

Run the live board on the day

A dark, glanceable Now / Next / Later board, built for a phone in low light. Blocks and shots update in real time across every device watching — no refreshing, no re-sending anything.

6

Everyone sees only their view

What each person sees — and can touch — is scoped to their role. That scoping is what this cycle of work was about, covered next.

This cycle's centerpiece

Three ways to see the same event

Everplan now works the way a small studio actually operates: one owner, and people around them with real, different permissions — not everyone looking at the same admin screen.

O

Owner

The event planner or lead photographer
  • Builds the timeline, by hand or with AI
  • Sees every shot, private or shared
  • Invites the team, sets up the guest QR link
  • Can preview the workspace as any other role
Unchanged from before — this cycle extended what everyone else can do around them.
T

Team

A second shooter, videographer, or content creator
  • Builds their own private shot list, with reference photos
  • Shares individual shots as a mood board — with the owner, or anyone else on the event
  • Checks off shots as captured, including ones shared by others
  • Can't edit or delete someone else's shots
"Private by default — only you see these."
R

Read-only

A vendor, coordinator, or client — invited by email
  • Sees the live timeline and anything shared with them
  • Revisits the same link any time — changes appear automatically
  • Can't create, edit, or check off anything
  • Never sees anyone's private shot list
"You are seeing the shots that have been shared with you."
What changed this cycle

From one admin view to real, enforced roles

Per-photographer shot lists

Previously only the event owner could create shots at all. Team members now build their own — the actual point of a photographer's shot list — and choose what to share, and with whom.

Genuinely read-only access

Vendor and client invites used to get the same access as a second shooter. They're now truly view-only: confirmed at the database level, not just by hiding buttons in the interface.

A "Client" role

Sharing a mood board with the couple, or a brand contact on a commercial shoot, now has its own label — separate from "Vendor" — same read-only access, clearer to everyone in the invite list.

Persistent account menu

Every signed-in screen now shows who you are — name, email, account settings, sign out — the way any Google product does. Previously this only half-existed on one screen.

"Preview as" for owners

An owner can now switch the workspace to render as Team or Read-only would see it, without losing their own access — a fast way to sanity-check what an invite is about to see before sending it.

Two real security gaps closed

Found and fixed while building this: any invited member could previously edit someone else's shared shot, and a stray database rule granted broader file-storage access than intended. Both are closed now.
